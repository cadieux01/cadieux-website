Everyone counts grams. Nobody counts what their body actually uses.

You can eat 30g of incomplete protein (amino acid profile is missing key links in the chain) and your body absorbs 18g of it effectively. Or eat 20g of complete protein (all nine essential amino acids present) and absorb 19g.

One sounds bigger. The other works better.

This is the difference between protein quantity and protein quality.

## What Makes Protein "Quality"

All protein is made of amino acids. There are 20 amino acids total. Your body can synthesize 11 of them. Nine are essential — you must eat them.

A "complete protein" has all nine essential amino acids in the right ratios.

- Whey protein: complete
- Eggs: complete
- Paneer: complete
- Chicken: complete
- Lentils alone: incomplete (missing methionine)
- Lentils + rice: complete (they complement each other)

Most animal proteins are complete. Plant proteins are often incomplete, but they pair (rice + beans, bread + peanut butter).

## Bioavailability: How Much Your Body Actually Uses

Eating protein and absorbing it are different things.

Whey protein isolate: 97–99% bioavailable. What you eat is almost entirely what your muscles get.

Whole egg: 97% bioavailable.

Cooked chicken: 93–98% bioavailable.

Lentils (unsoaked, undercooked): 60–75% bioavailable. The same grams of protein deliver less to your muscles because anti-nutrients block absorption.

Fermented bread (sourdough): increases bioavailability of amino acids because fermentation breaks down phytic acid and partially pre-digests the protein.

So: 20g of whey ≈ 19.4g usable. 30g of undercooked lentils ≈ 18–22.5g usable. Same math different ways.

## Why This Matters

Your goal is: amino acids reaching muscle tissue, not amino acids sitting in your digestive system being poorly absorbed.

A supplement company sees "30g protein per serving." You should see "how much of that 30g will my body actually use?"

## The Practical Test

Eat 30g of protein from mediocre bread. Eat 20g from quality bread (complete protein, fermented, bioavailable).

Track how you feel 3 hours later. Which one delivered satiety? Which one kept energy stable?

Your body knows the difference even if the label doesn't show it.

Quality protein is more expensive. It requires longer fermentation, better ingredients, actual care. It's worth it because your body responds faster and stronger to quality.

The question isn't "how much protein?" It's "how much of that protein does my body actually use?"

Start counting that instead. Everything else follows.
