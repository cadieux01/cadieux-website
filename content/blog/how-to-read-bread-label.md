Bread labels lie without lying. They tell the truth in a way that makes bad bread sound good.

Here's how to read past the hype.

## Start With Serving Size

The label says "protein: 5g." Sound good? Check the serving size first.

Most mass-market bread lists a serving as one slice. Some brands list a serving as "1/2 slice" or "2/3 of a slice." They're not trying to be helpful. They're trying to make the nutrition look better.

Cadieux is simple: one slice is one serving. No tricks.

## The Protein-to-Carb Ratio

Divide protein by carbs.

A slice of regular white bread might be 2g protein and 25g carbs. That's 0.08. You're eating mostly carbs.

A slice of high-protein bread should be 6g protein and 20g carbs. That's 0.3. You're eating more protein than carb.

The ratio matters more than the absolute number.

## Watch for "Sugar" Hiding

Ingredient list shows no added sugar? Check for:

- Honey
- Agave
- Maple syrup
- Molasses
- Malt extract
- Brown rice syrup

These are sugars with marketing names. If the label says "no added sugar" but the ingredient list has three of these, the manufacturer is being technically true and practically dishonest.

Fermented bread doesn't need sugar. It ferments into sweetness naturally.

## Fiber Tricks

A label claims "8g fiber!" It might actually say "5g fiber" but counted "3g chicory inulin" (added fiber) separately.

Added fiber has its place, but it's not the same as fiber that comes from the actual grain structure.

Good bread has fiber from ingredients: whole grains, seeds, bran. Not from "added chicory inulin."

Check: are the first two ingredients refined flour or whole grain?

If refined flour is first, you're eating white bread with good marketing.

## The Sodium Trap

Bread needs salt. Salt is good. But some brands use way too much.

Cadieux has about 2% of your daily sodium per slice. Some breads hit 5–7% per slice. If you eat two slices, you're already at your daily limit.

Higher sodium makes bread taste more interesting and stay fresher longer (salt is a preservative). But your blood pressure doesn't care about the reason.

Check sodium. Compare across brands. 350mg per slice is normal. 500mg+ is aggressive.

## Ingredient Order: The Truth List

Ingredients are listed by weight. The first ingredient is what you're mostly eating.

"Bread" with "wheat flour" as the first ingredient is mostly carbs. "Bread" with "whey protein isolate" as the second ingredient after flour still has flour as the main ingredient.

A good protein bread has wheat flour first (it's still bread), but protein source in the top three.

## The "No Preservatives" Claim

If a bread promises long shelf life and says "no preservatives," it's using a trick.

Common tricks:
- Vinegar (natural preservative, acidifies)
- Citric acid (natural, but it's still a preservative)
- Enzyme cocktails (marketed as "natural" but still additives)
- High sugar (keeps bread fresh by osmotic pressure)

There's nothing wrong with these. But "no preservatives" is misleading if the bread still lasts three weeks on a shelf.

Real bread with no preservatives or tricks? 4–5 days shelf life. That's it.

## Fermentation: The Word to Trust

If the label says "sourdough fermented" or "natural starter" or "overnight fermented," you're looking at bread that put in time.

If it says "rapid-rise" or "quick-bake" or "ready in 4 hours," it's industrial bread. Not necessarily bad. Just different.

Fermentation matters because it breaks down anti-nutrients and makes amino acids more bioavailable. A label that mentions fermentation is honest about the process.

## The Test That Never Lies

Read the label. Check the ratio. Verify the fermentation. Then buy a loaf and eat it.

How do you feel 2 hours later? Steady energy or a crash? Satiated or hungry?

Your body doesn't read labels. It reads how the bread actually performs.

Trust your experience more than you trust the label. The label is just a starting point.
