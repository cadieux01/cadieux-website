Protein fills you. Fiber keeps you full.

They're different jobs. But when they arrive in the same bite, something shifts.

## How They Work Separately

Protein signals fullness to your brain. The message: "you're fed, stop eating." Lasts 2–3 hours.

Fiber slows digestion. The message: "this food is taking time to break down, energy is arriving steadily." Lasts 3–4 hours.

Eat 20g protein alone (whey shake): you're full, but hunger creeps back at hour 3. Eat 10g fiber alone (bowl of broccoli): you get fullness, but it's psychological not nutritional.

Eat 15g protein + 8g fiber in the same food (Cadieux loaf): you get both messages simultaneously. Fullness that lasts 4+ hours because digestion is slow and the brain is saying "still fed."

## The Glucose Angle

Protein slows glucose absorption. Fiber slows glucose absorption. Together, they're powerful.

A slice of regular white bread hits your bloodstream fast. Your glucose spikes. Your body dumps insulin to manage it. Glucose crashes. You're hungry 90 minutes later.

A slice of Cadieux bread: protein slows the spike, fiber slows it more. Glucose rises steadily. Insulin response is mild. You're stable for 3+ hours. No crash. No hunger.

This is the difference between eating bread that spikes your blood sugar and bread that doesn't.

## Why Food Companies Don't Do This

Fiber and protein are expensive to combine. Protein sources are generally expensive. High-fiber ingredients (whole grains, seeds, bran) add cost and change texture.

Most industrial bread is: cheap white flour (fast carbs) + maybe 2g protein from the flour itself. That's it. Easy to make. Cheap to produce. Spikes your glucose every single time.

Adding serious protein AND serious fiber means higher price, more careful process, longer fermentation.

It's cheaper to sell mediocre bread that leaves you hungry and hungry again at 10 AM. Your repeated purchases are the business model.
