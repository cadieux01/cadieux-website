Everyone asks the same question: where do you get protein without chicken getting boring?

The answer isn't another meat. It's understanding that protein density isn't confined to animal sources. It's distributed across foods you already know.

Ranked by practical utility for people who train:

## 1. Whey Protein Isolate (30g per serving)

Not a food. But the reference. Whey is the fastest-absorbing protein, complete amino acid profile, already separated from lactose and fat. The baseline that everything else competes against.

Used in Cadieux because it delivers what the body needs immediately after a workout.

## 2. Eggs (6g per egg)

The perfect protein. Complete amino acids, bioavailable, affordable, sustainable, ready in 4 minutes. Two eggs hit 12g. Three hit 18g.

Eggs aren't boring. They're just underutilized. Soft-boiled. Scrambled. Baked into bread. Fried into rice. The problem isn't eggs — it's repetition of how you cook them.

## 3. Paneer (25g per 100g)

Indian strength standard. High-density, absorbs quickly, cooks in 10 minutes. Pairs with bread, rice, any carb. The cost per gram is better than chicken.

Paneer butter masala with two slices of high-protein bread is a complete meal.

## 4. Lentils/Dal (9g per cooked cup)

Underrated. Lentils are complete plant protein when paired with rice or grain. Cheaper than anything except eggs. Cook in bulk, use all week.

Red lentils vanish into dal makhani. Brown lentils hold shape in salads. The amino acid profile isn't perfect solo, but paired with bread or rice, it's complete.

## 5. Greek Yogurt (10g per 100g)

Thicker. Creamier. Higher protein than regular yogurt. Absorbs slower than whey (better for satiety). Pairs with breakfast or as a snack.

Ingredient-check Greek yogurt. Some brands add stabilizers and sugars. The good ones are just milk and cultures.

## 6. Sesame Seeds (9g per 30g serving)

Overlooked. Sesame seeds are dense in protein and healthy fat. Sprinkle on salads. Mix into dressings. Use as a bread topping.

Don't expect them to be a standalone protein source. Use them as a multiplier on other meals.

## 7. Soy Protein (20g per 50g serving)

Plant-based alternative to whey. Slower absorption, but complete amino acids. Useful if you're lactose-sensitive or philosophically opposed to dairy.

Soy chunks (textured soy protein) are cheap and cook quickly. Soy flour integrates into baked goods.

## 8. Chicken (35g per breast)

Yes, still here. Chicken isn't boring — the way people cook it is. Poached is boring. Pan-seared with garlic is different. Shredded into biryani is different.

High protein, low fat, affordable, available everywhere. The fact that everyone defaults to it doesn't mean it's wrong.

## 9. Fish (25g per 100g)

Faster-absorbing than chicken. Omega-3 profile is stronger. Sardines are cheap and shelf-stable. Salmon has fat that carries fat-soluble vitamins. Tuna is lean.

Fish isn't accessible to everyone, but when available, it's competitive with chicken on nutrition and better on the "whole food" spectrum.

## The Actual Point

Protein doesn't require chicken. It requires density, repetition, and pairing. Eggs are cheaper and faster. Paneer is underused. Lentils are complete when paired with grain.

Bread is the carrier. Cadieux exists because protein makes sense on bread. Two slices with paneer or eggs or lentils isn't a "protein snack." It's a meal. One of several you'll have that day. Each one built on a protein source that works for your routine.

The question isn't "where do I get protein?" It's "which protein source fits the meal I'm about to eat?"

Answer that, and everything else solves itself.
