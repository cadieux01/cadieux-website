You don't have time to meal prep. You don't have time to think about what you eat. You have time for breakfast, lunch, and maybe a coffee.

Eating well isn't about perfection. It's about building it into your routine so it requires zero additional thinking.

## The Principle: Same Thing, Every Time

Your morning is full. Your commute is full. Your 9-to-5 is full. Your evening is full.

But breakfast is at the same time, every day. Lunch is around the same time. Coffee is at the same time.

Don't optimize around variety. Optimize around repetition.

Eat the same breakfast every day for three months. Your body adapts. Your energy stabilizes. Your brain stops asking "what should I eat?" because the answer is already decided.

Same breakfast: two slices of Cadieux bread, two eggs scrambled or fried, butter.

That's it. Every day. Same 30g protein. Same satiety window. No thinking. 4 minutes.

Your brain's bandwidth isn't needed. You can use it for actual work.

## Lunch: No Cooking Required

You can't cook at work. So lunch isn't about cooking. It's about assembly.

Pre-made rotisserie chicken from the grocery store. Leftover paneer from yesterday. Canned tuna. Store-bought dal.

Take one of these. Put it on two slices of bread. Add butter or mayo if you want. Eat at your desk.

20–30 minutes before your actual work day starts: done with lunch.

This doesn't require meal prep. It requires buying food that's already prepared and showing up.

## Coffee: The Non-Meal Eating Moment

At 3 PM, your energy dips. Everyone around you is reaching for pastries or cookies or caffeine.

Your move: coffee + one slice of Cadieux bread with peanut butter.

That's 4g coffee, 8g protein from bread, 8g from peanut butter. 16g of protein lands while everyone else is crashing.

You're steady at 4 PM when they're hunting for snacks.

No complexity. No special timing. Just coffee + bread, which is the moment everyone's already sitting down for coffee.

## Dinner: Doesn't Have to Be Effort

You're tired at dinner. No one wants to cook.

Order takeout. Yes, really. Add bread to it.

Biryani + Cadieux bread. Paneer butter masala + Cadieux bread. Dhal + rice + Cadieux bread.

The bread isn't the meal. The bread is the protein boost that makes takeout actually nutritious instead of empty calories.

You're not cooking. You're not meal prepping. You're just adding one component that makes the rest of your nutrition lock in.

## The Math for Busy People

Breakfast: 30g protein (eggs + bread)
Lunch: 25g protein (pre-made protein + bread)
3 PM: 16g protein (bread + pb + coffee)
Dinner: 20g protein (takeout + bread)
Total: 91g protein

Most people eating the same meals hit 40–50g. You're hitting 90g without adding time to your day.

The difference isn't effort. It's knowing what to eat and having it ready.

## One More Thing

You don't have time to eat well because you've bought the narrative that eating well requires time.

It doesn't. It requires one decision (what's my breakfast every day?) and then habit does the work.

Everything else follows.

You eat the same breakfast every day for three months. Your body responds. Your energy is stable. Your hunger is predictable. Your afternoon doesn't include the 3 PM crash.

Eating well isn't about having time. It's about not wasting time on decisions that don't matter.

Decide breakfast. Decide lunch. Decide the protein. Then stop thinking.

Let the routine do the work.
